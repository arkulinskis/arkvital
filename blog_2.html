Looking for problems for AI to solve

A new tool can be exciting before we know what to do with it. That’s a reason to experiment—and a reason to stay curious about the problem.

Every powerful new technology creates a period of exuberance. People try it everywhere, including in places where the old method is already working. It can look like a solution searching for a problem.

But experimentation is part of how we learn what a technology is actually good for. Early uses are often clumsy. They reveal what is possible, where the limits are, and which assumptions deserve a second look.

Keep the problem in the room

The useful question isn’t simply whether AI can be added to a task. It is whether the task matters, what a better outcome looks like, and whether the tool can help us reach it safely and reliably. Sometimes the experiment shows that a simpler approach is still best. Sometimes it opens a route that wasn’t practical before.

That distinction matters in business. A clever demo is not the same as a dependable process. Consider the people who will rely on the result, the quality of the information going in, and what happens when the system gets it wrong. Use experiments to learn, then judge the outcome against the real work.

Curiosity moves technology forward. Clear problems help curiosity turn into value.
