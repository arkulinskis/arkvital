Don’t dismiss the silly idea

What looks unnecessary to one person might bring joy, identity, or just the right kind of surprise to someone else.

Some successful products look absurd when you describe them plainly. A blanket with sleeves. A rock sold as a pet. A tiny video that makes you laugh on a busy day. It is tempting to judge an idea by whether it seems useful to us.

But usefulness isn’t only speed or convenience. People choose things for delight, belonging, expression, comfort, and many other reasons. A product can meet a need that its creator didn’t expect—or that a spreadsheet doesn’t capture.

Get curious about the person

That doesn’t mean every unconventional idea is secretly brilliant. It means a quick dismissal can close off learning. Ask who might love it, what moment it fits into, and what feeling or job it serves. Then put a small version in front of real people and listen.

Customers may not describe the solution before it exists. They can still show us what they value when they encounter it. The product team’s job is to notice the signal, including when it comes from a surprising place.

So keep a little room for the playful, the strange, and the not-quite-practical. Sometimes the odd idea is only odd until you understand who it is for.
